# OKLCH color space

How we describe colors: lightness, chroma, hue — and why that makes theming so simple. Every color token of the theme is an `oklch()` value.

## In short

OKLCH describes a color the way we see it: how light, how colorful, which hue. The same number means the same impression — for every hue.

<OklchAxes />

```css
color: oklch(0.52 0.255 275); /* L  C  H – the indigo accent of the Dextinity brand */
```

In the theme, the same accent is `--dextinity-color-accent: oklch(0.52 var(--dextinity-brand-primary-chroma) var(--dextinity-brand-primary-hue))`: the color scheme sets the lightness, the brand the chroma and the hue.

## Why it matters to us

- **Predictable contrast.** The same L means a similar contrast. White text on the accent works for indigo, orange, and violet with the same rule — instead of measuring color by color.
- **Clean scales.** Our primitives, `--dextinity-color-<scale>-<step>`, have 11 steps (50–950) on a fixed lightness curve. Step 500 is equally light in every scale, so steps are interchangeable.
- **Theming with one number.** At its core, a brand is a hue and a chroma (`--dextinity-brand-primary-hue`, `--dextinity-brand-primary-chroma`). All states, surfaces, and gradients are computed from them.
- **States without tables.** A soft tint or a lighter variant shifts L and scales C, e.g. `--dextinity-color-accent-soft` is the accent hue at L 0.955 with a fifth of the chroma. No hex value of its own per state and brand.
- **Dark and high contrast.** The color schemes mostly turn L, and lower C a little where the color gets light. Hue stays, so the brand looks the same in every color scheme.
- **More colors.** OKLCH can also describe Display P3, the wider color space of modern screens. Older devices automatically get the nearest sRGB color.

## Limits

- **Too colorful doesn't fit everywhere.** High chroma at very light or very dark lies outside sRGB and gets clipped. That's why the primitives' chroma curve lowers C toward the ends of the scales. A brand with a high chroma, such as Dextinity's 0.255, still leaves sRGB in the light steps — the demo above marks them.
- **Figma doesn't speak OKLCH.** The primitives are computed and transferred to Figma as hex. The source of truth is the OKLCH values in the code.
- **Check contrast anyway.** L is a very good approximation, but doesn't replace the WCAG measurement. The [Colors](?path=/docs/foundations-colors--docs) page computes it live.
